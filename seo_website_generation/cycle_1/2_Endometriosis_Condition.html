What is Endometriosis?

Endometriosis is a chronic condition where tissue similar to the lining of the uterus (endometrium) grows outside the uterus. This tissue can implant on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, intestines, and pelvic lining. Because this displaced tissue behaves like normal endometrial tissue—thickening, breaking down, and bleeding with each menstrual cycle—it becomes trapped, leading to severe inflammation, scar tissue (adhesions), and cysts known as endometriomas.

Recognizing the Symptoms

Many women suffer in silence for years before receiving an accurate diagnosis. Look out for these key signs:

  • Dysmenorrhea: Pelvic pain and cramping that begins before and extends several days into a menstrual period.
  • Pain with Intercourse: Deep pain during or after sex is a hallmark symptom.
  • Pain with Bowel Movements or Urination: Especially during your period.
  • Infertility: Sometimes, endometriosis is only discovered when seeking Infertility Treatment.

How is Endometriosis Diagnosed?

Diagnosis begins with a detailed medical history and pelvic exam. Advanced pelvic ultrasounds can identify large cysts (endometriomas). However, the gold standard for definitive diagnosis and staging of the disease is a diagnostic Laparoscopic Surgery.

Treatment Options: Medical vs. Surgical

Our approach is highly individualized, depending on the severity of your symptoms and your family planning goals.

Medical Management

Hormonal therapies, including birth control pills, progestin therapy, or GnRH agonists, can slow the growth of endometrial tissue and prevent new implants.

Conservative Surgery

If you are trying to get pregnant or experiencing excruciating pain, conservative laparoscopic surgery to excise (cut out) the endometriosis implants while preserving your uterus and ovaries is recommended.

Frequently Asked Questions About Endometriosis

Does endometriosis go away after menopause?

For many women, the symptoms of endometriosis improve significantly after menopause because the displaced endometrial tissue is no longer stimulated by the monthly hormone cycles. However, exceptions do occur, especially if hormone replacement therapy is used.

Can I still get pregnant if I have endometriosis?

Yes. While endometriosis can make it harder to conceive, many women with mild to moderate disease conceive naturally. For more advanced stages, treatments like laparoscopic excision or IVF Treatment are highly effective.
